# LumaDesign Business Dashboard: first-meeting questionnaire

*For the first meeting with the owner, 21 November 2026. Goes with [BUSINESS-DASHBOARD-PROPOSAL.md](BUSINESS-DASHBOARD-PROPOSAL.md).*

**How to use it:**
- **Essential** questions (23 in total) shape the design. Aim to answer all of them in a 45–60 minute meeting.
- **Optional** questions can be answered later, by message or in a second meeting.
- "I don't know yet" is a fine answer. It tells us to keep that part simple or flexible.
- No figures are needed in the meeting itself. Where a question asks about amounts, a rough idea is enough; exact numbers are entered later in the portal.

## 2. Products and costs

### Essential

**2.1 Are the lamps made by you, bought ready-made, or both?** If both, roughly what share of each?

*Why it matters:* a lamp you make is costed from its parts and labour. A lamp you buy is costed from the supplier price plus transport. The cost screens are built differently for each.

> Answer:

**2.2 What goes into the cost of one custom lamp?** Tick what applies and add anything missing.
☐ Shade / body ☐ Finish (paint, plating) ☐ Cord ☐ Bulbs ☐ Canopy and fittings ☐ Assembly labour ☐ Packaging ☐ Delivery ☐ Other: ______

*Why it matters:* the lamp designer already prices a custom lamp from shade size, cord and bulbs. The cost side needs the same building blocks, so each order can show its real profit.

> Answer:

**2.3 Do different designs of the same size cost different amounts to make?** Or is the cost mainly decided by size and finish?

*Why it matters:* if the cost depends only on size and finish, one short price list covers every design. If each design differs, each design needs its own base cost. This is the biggest factor in how much typing costs will take.

> Answer:

**2.4 How often do your costs change, and do you know when they change?** For example: the brass supplier raises prices twice a year.

*Why it matters:* each cost is saved with the date it started. Old orders keep the cost they had, so past profit never changes when today's prices change.

> Answer:

**2.5 Do the finishes (ivory, black, brass) cost different amounts?** Should customers pay more for some finishes?

*Why it matters:* today the website price is the same for every finish. If costs differ, profit per lamp will differ too, and you may want a surcharge.

> Answer:

### Optional

**2.6** Do you keep stock of parts or finished lamps? If yes, how do you track it today?

*Why it matters:* stock tracking is a later, optional phase. It is only worth building if stock causes real problems (running out, money tied up).

**2.7** Do you have a minimum profit margin you aim for on each lamp?

*Why it matters:* the dashboard can warn you when a price falls below it.

## 3. Expenses

### Essential

**3.1 What are your main regular expenses?** Tick what applies.
☐ Rent ☐ Salaries / wages ☐ Electricity, water, internet ☐ Materials and parts ☐ Delivery / transport ☐ Marketing and ads ☐ Software and subscriptions ☐ Maintenance ☐ Other: ______

*Why it matters:* this becomes the starting list of expense categories. A short, familiar list makes entry quick and reports clear.

> Answer:

**3.2 Where are expenses recorded today?** Accounting software (which one?), a spreadsheet, a notebook, bank statements only, or not recorded?

*Why it matters:* if you already use accounting software, the dashboard should **not** become a second set of books. We would import totals or record only what the dashboard needs. If nothing is used today, the portal becomes the place to record expenses.

> Answer:

**3.3 Who would enter expenses, and how often?** You, an accountant, a staff member; daily, weekly, monthly?

*Why it matters:* it decides who needs access to expenses and whether entry should be quick on a phone (photo of the receipt) or done in batches on a computer.

> Answer:

**3.4 Do you keep receipts, and would you like them stored with each expense?**

*Why it matters:* receipts are stored privately with the expense. They use free storage space, so photos are shrunk automatically.

> Answer:

### Optional

**3.5** Are materials bought in bulk (for many lamps) or per order?

*Why it matters:* bulk purchases are counted once, as each lamp is sold, so they are not subtracted twice from profit.

**3.6** Do you have an accountant who will need reports? In what format?

## 4. Sales and payments

### Essential

**4.1 When does a sale "count" for you?** When the customer confirms, when they pay a deposit, when the lamp is delivered, or when it is fully paid?

*Why it matters:* this is the most important definition in the whole dashboard. The proposal suggests: an order counts as "booked" when confirmed and as "revenue" when delivered. Money received is shown separately. We will follow what makes sense for you and your accountant.

> Answer:

**4.2 How do customers pay?** Tick what applies.
☐ Cash ☐ Bank transfer ☐ Mobile wallet ☐ Card (machine) ☐ Instapay ☐ Other: ______

*Why it matters:* this becomes the list of payment methods, so you can see where money arrived and reconcile it with the bank.

> Answer:

**4.3 Do customers pay a deposit and then the balance?** Typical split?

*Why it matters:* if yes, each order needs several payments and the dashboard shows who still owes money.

> Answer:

**4.4 Is the business registered for VAT? Do your prices include VAT?**

*Why it matters:* sales and profit must be shown without VAT, since VAT is not your money. If prices include VAT, the system separates it automatically.

> Answer:

**4.5 Do you charge customers for delivery or installation?** Is it a fixed fee, by area, or included in the price?

*Why it matters:* delivery charged to the customer is kept apart from lamp sales, so the profit on lamps stays clear.

> Answer:

**4.6 Roughly how many orders do you handle in a normal month?** A range is enough (for example: under 10, 10–50, more than 50).

*Why it matters:* it decides how much the order screens need search and filters, and confirms the free database is more than enough.

> Answer:

### Optional

**4.7** How often do you give discounts? Who decides them?

**4.8** How often are orders cancelled or returned? What happens to the deposit?

**4.9** What steps does an order go through between confirmation and delivery (for example: in production, ready, out for delivery)?

*Why it matters:* the order statuses should match how work really flows.

## 5. Marketing and customers

### Essential

**5.1 Do you spend money on advertising?** Where (Instagram, Facebook, Google, printed, events)?

*Why it matters:* if you advertise, recording the spend lets the dashboard show what it costs to win a customer. If not, that report is left out.

> Answer:

**5.2 Do you keep a customer list today?** Where (phone contacts, spreadsheet, WhatsApp)? Would you like past customers imported?

*Why it matters:* customers are matched by phone number so the same person is not created twice. An existing list can be imported once to start with history.

> Answer:

### Optional

**5.3** How do customers usually hear about you?

*Why it matters:* the website can count where visitors come from (search, Instagram, WhatsApp) without cookies or personal data. Word of mouth needs a "how did you hear about us" question when the order is recorded.

**5.4** Do you work with interior designers or partners who earn a commission?

**5.5** Would you like the website to count visits, popular designs and quote requests? This is anonymous: no cookies and no personal data. The privacy page will be updated to say so.
